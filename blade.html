Chinese Weapon Arts

The blade built to end a cavalry charge

The zhan ma dao is not a sword. It is a long-handled blade with its mass parked forward of the hand — designed so that a single descending stroke takes the horse out from under the rider.

The weapon

Long blade, short distance, weight out front

The form is recorded in the Ming military compendium Wubei Zhi: a wide-edged blade on a long shaft, gripped low, carried with the point angled down. Against armour it is a poor weapon. Against legs, it is decisive.

Grip lowBoth hands sit at the far end of the shaft. The blade's weight is not held, it is steered.
Mass forwardMost of the weight lives past the guard. The stroke falls almost on its own once it starts.
One arcNo recovery between strokes. Each form is the beginning of the next one.
The sequence

Ten forms, one continuous path

Rebuilt in slow motion from a modern motion-capture demonstration of the blade, then written down as a numbered path. These are the forms as performed today, not the Ming originals — the names are traditional, the choreography is contemporary.

I. Opening

the blade leaves the hand before it can be used
01

Push & Release

推刀脱手

The blade is pushed clear of the body and the grip is opened. Nothing is being hit yet — the point is to load the next beat.

02

Spin & Catch

转身接刀

A half turn of the body brings the hand to where the handle will fall, not where it currently is. Timing, not speed.

03

Overhead Cleave

力劈华山

The whole mass drops from above the shoulder to the ground. The first strike that actually lands.

II. Advance

low entry, high exit — find the gap under the guard
04

Side Turn, Blade Rises

侧身起刀

The body turns side-on so the blade can travel upward through the centre line without the torso blocking it.

05

Bow Stance Rising Cut

弓步上撩

Front leg loaded, weight forward, the edge running up from knee height to chin height.

06

Step In, Return Edge

上步回锋

The step closes the distance the rising cut opened, and the back edge comes home on the return.

III. Turn

the body becomes the axis of the blade
07

Whirl & Body Turn

抡刀转身

The blade is spun around the body's vertical axis. Long weapons turn like this — the body travels, the hands stay.

08

Bow Stance Flat Slash

弓步横斩

The whirl lands as a horizontal cut at waist height. Everything gathered in step 07 is spent here.

IV. Close

the final stroke is a throw, not a cut
09

Kick to Borrow Momentum

踢刀借力

The foot strikes the shaft to add rotation the arms could not generate on their own.

10

Airborne Launch

腾空飞刀

The blade leaves the hands entirely. The sequence ends with the weapon in the air and the body still.

Why it reads as one motion. Nothing here depends on arm strength. Every form hands its momentum to the next one — the fall of the cleave feeds the rise of the cut, the rise feeds the whirl, the whirl feeds the throw. Break the chain and the blade outruns you.
